    <div class="section-head text-center">
      <span class="section-kicker">Information for beginners</span>
      <h1 class="section-title">Become a radio amateur</h1>
      <p class="section-sub">
        Transmitting yourself, making contacts worldwide and building your own
        equipment — an operator certificate and a station license allows it.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="row justify-content-center">
      <div class="col-lg-9">
        <div class="prose">
          <p>
            As a radio amateur you have wide latitude to transmit and to build or modify
            your own transmitting equipment. Doing that safely and responsibly takes
            technical and operational know-how, plus a grasp of the regulations.
            That is why the <strong>BIPT</strong> (the Belgian Institute for Postal
            Services and Telecommunications) organizes radio amateur exams.
          </p>
          <p>
            Passing earns you an <strong>operator certificate</strong>. With it you may
            transmit, and you can apply for a <strong>station license</strong> to set up
            and operate your own radio station. No prior knowledge of electronics is
            needed: the courses in the UBA sections start from scratch.
          </p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

    <h2 class="vd-subhead" id="klassen">The three classes</h2>
    <div class="row justify-content-center">
      <div class="col-lg-9">
        <div class="prose">
          <p>
            There are three classes of operator certificate. The more privileges you get,
            the more demanding the exam. Class C is the entry level (the "basic license");
            class A grants full privileges.
          </p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="row justify-content-center">
      <div class="col-lg-9">
        <div class="prose">
          <p>
            The exam consists of <strong>multiple-choice questions</strong> with four
            possible answers, one of which is correct. Every correct answer counts for
            1 point; a wrong or blank answer for 0 points. There is 
            <strong>no penalty for guessing</strong> — always answer every question,
            even if you are unsure.
          </p>
          <p>
            <strong>No exemptions whatsoever</strong> are granted: not on the basis of
            other qualifications, and not because you previously passed particular parts
            of the exam.
          </p>
          <p>
            The syllabus for the technical and procedural parts follows the CEPT recommendations
            (TR&nbsp;61-02 for class A, ECC&nbsp;(05)06 for class B and ECC Report 89 for
            class C). As a result the syllabus is similar in all CEPT countries, which is
            what makes mutual recognition of licenses possible. The regulations part is
            set by the BIPT and is identical for all three exams.
          </p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

    <h2 class="vd-subhead" id="bipt">Taking the exam at the BIPT in Brussels</h2>
    <div class="row justify-content-center">
      <div class="col-lg-9">
        <div class="prose">
          <p>
            The BIPT holds the exams <strong>at its own offices in Brussels</strong>:
          </p>
          <address class="bipt-address">
            BIPT<br>
            Koning Albert II-laan 32 bus 10<br>
            1000 Brussels<br>
            tel. <a href="tel:+3222268888">02 226 88 88</a> ·
            <a href="mailto:info@bipt.be">info@bipt.be</a>
          </address>
          <p>
            By arrangement with a recognized radio amateur association, an exam can also
            take place elsewhere; those sessions are announced on the UBA website. Exams
            are held <strong>on business days during office hours only</strong>.
          </p>
          <h3>Registering</h3>
          <p>
            You register by email at
            <a href="mailto:examen@bipt.be">examen@bipt.be</a>, stating:
          </p>
          <ul>
            <li>your first name and surname as they appear on your identity card;</li>
            <li>your full address;</li>
            <li>your phone number (not required, but useful);</li>
            <li>the exam you are registering for (class A, B or C);</li>
            <li>
              the period in which you want to take the exam — or the place and date if you
              are registering for a session at another location.
            </li>
          </ul>
          <p>As attachments you add:</p>
          <ul>
            <li>a copy of your identity card (front and back);</li>
            <li>a copy of your practical test certificate — for class C only.</li>
          </ul>
          <p>
            The BIPT then sends a confirmation with the date and time, together with an
            invitation to pay the registration fee. <strong>Your registration is only
            complete once that payment has been made.</strong>
          </p>
          <p>
            You must be <strong>at least 12 years old</strong> to take an exam.
          </p>
          <h3>On the day</h3>
          <p>
            Be on time: if you arrive less than 15 minutes before the exam starts, the
            BIPT may refuse you entry.
          </p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

    <h2 class="vd-subhead" id="praktische-proef">The practical test (class C)</h2>
    <div class="row justify-content-center">
      <div class="col-lg-9">
        <div class="prose">
          <p>
            To take the BIPT class C exam you must first have passed the
            <strong>practical test</strong>. It is administered by the recognized radio
            amateur associations, usually at the end of a class C course — the course
            also prepares you for it.
          </p>
          <p>
            During the test you demonstrate that you can operate an amateur radio station
            correctly and make a contact following the proper procedures.
          </p>
          <p>
            For the class A and class B exams you do <strong>not</strong> need this
            certificate.
          </p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

    <h2 class="vd-subhead" id="morseproef">The Morse test</h2>
    <div class="row justify-content-center">
      <div class="col-lg-9">
        <div class="prose">
          <blockquote>
            <strong>Morse is no longer required.</strong> You do not need to know Morse
            to obtain an operator certificate or license in Belgium — CW is not part of
            the class A, B or C exams.
          </blockquote>
          <p>
            The Morse test does still exist, but it is entirely <strong>voluntary</strong>.
            Those who pass receive a <em>certificate of achievement</em>. Holders of a
            Belgian <strong>class A</strong> operator certificate can use it to have the
            endorsement <strong>"Morse xx WPM"</strong> added to their certificate.
          </p>
          <p>
            In Flanders the BIPT Morse test is organized by
            <strong>UBA section DST (Diest)</strong>
             — in 2026 for the ninth time, in accordance with the decision of the Council
            of the BIPT of January 19, 2021.
          </p>
          <h3>Two levels</h3>
          <p>
            Two tests are held one after the other, at different sending speeds:

            Both tests take place on the same day, one after the other, so you can go for
            the higher level if you wish. The <a href="https://www.on4dst.be/cw_proef%20info.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ON4DST website</a> has the current date

            Radio amateurs from abroad may take part; validating the certificate is then
            up to their own administration.
          </p>

    <h2 class="vd-subhead" id="voorbereiden">Preparing</h2>
    <div class="row justify-content-center">
      <div class="col-lg-9">
        <div class="prose">
          <ul>
            <li>
              <strong>Textbooks.</strong> For classes A and B there is the
              <em>UBA HAREC handbook</em>; for class C the
              <em>UBA basic license handbook</em>. Both are available from the UBA
              service bureau.
            </li>
            <li>
              <strong>Courses in the UBA sections.</strong> A class C course usually
              comprises 12 to 20 teaching hours spread over a few weeks. Class A is a
              good deal more: 30 to 40 sessions of 2 to 3 hours (80 to 120 teaching
              hours), spread over six months to a year. Class B sits in between. In most
              cases no prior knowledge is required.
            </li>
            <li>
              <strong>The UBA online learning environment.</strong> With practice
              questions per class — roughly 800 questions for class A (plus a mock exam),
              400 for class B and 250 for class C — and forums for your questions about
              the syllabus.
            </li>
          </ul>
          <p>
            <strong>Not sure where to start?</strong> Drop by one of our meetings —
            you will find the dates and places on the
            <a href="{% include url.html slug='home' lang='en' %}{{ page_url | relative_url }}#vergaderen">home page</a>. We are happy to
            help you choose a class and prepare for it, whether you already have
            experience with electronics or are starting from zero.
          </p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
